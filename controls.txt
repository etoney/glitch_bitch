===============================================================================
  GLITCH WITCH  //  CONTROLS QUICK REFERENCE
  Castle Danger Music Company  //  Press. Hold. Destroy.
===============================================================================

Glitch Witch is an insert effect. Put it on any track (drums, vocals, a bus,
the whole mix) like you would an EQ or a reverb.

The idea: ARM the effects you want, then hit THE BUTTON. Everything armed
fires at once. Every glitch is also recorded as a WAV file, with a little
clean audio before and after it, and lands in the CAPTURE TRAY so you can
drag it onto its own track.

Tip: double-click a value readout under a knob to type an exact number.
Double-click a knob to reset it.

No latency: Glitch Witch doesn't delay your audio.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HEADER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  THE SCREEN (top left)
      Your output scrolling by. It tears apart while the glitch is firing,
      and shows WAITING FOR THE GRID when SNAP is holding a press back.

  PRESET MENU (top right)
      17 factory glitches, plus your own saved presets under USER PRESETS.
      The < and > arrows step through both lists.

      Init (Stutter 1/16)  Chopper 1/32       Gate Keeper        Beat Repeat 1/8
      Machine Gun          Sinking Repeat     Rising Panic       Tape Kill
      Spin Cycle           Power Up           Backmask           Scatterbrain
      Broken CD            8-Bit Meltdown     Frozen Scream      Death Dive
      Total Destruction

      Presets change the effects and SNAP. They never touch THE BUTTON, the
      HOLD/LATCH mode, the capture settings or the OUTPUT level.

  SAVE PRESET... / DELETE
      User presets are .glitchwitch files you can back up or share:
        macOS:   ~/Library/Application Support/Castle Danger/Glitch Witch/Presets/
        Windows: %APPDATA%\Castle Danger\Glitch Witch\Presets\


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE BUTTON  (left)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  THE BUTTON   Fires every ARMED effect. It glows while it's firing.

  MODE         HOLD:  fires while you hold the button down. Let go to stop.
               LATCH: click once to start, click again to stop.

  SNAP TO GRID Off, 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1 Bar.
               With SNAP on, a press waits for the next grid line before it
               fires, and a release waits for the next grid line before it
               stops. Even a quick tap fires for one full grid step. This
               keeps glitches on the beat, and it makes every captured clip
               start exactly on a grid line (see CAPTURE TRAY).
               Snap only works while the song is playing.

  ARMED        Lists what will fire when you hit the button.

  Recording and controlling the button from your DAW:
      THE BUTTON is a normal plugin parameter called "Glitch (The Button)".
        - Record it: put the track's automation in Touch, Latch or Write
          mode, play, and press the button. Your presses become automation
          you can edit and play back.
        - Draw it: draw the "Glitch (The Button)" automation lane by hand.
        - Map it to a pad, key or footswitch: use your DAW's controller
          mapping (in Fender Studio Pro: Control Link / "Learn").
      Every ARM switch is a parameter too, so you can change which effects
      fire from one glitch to the next.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE EFFECTS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each plate has an ARM switch (top right). Armed plates have a red outline
and light up while the glitch fires. Arm as many as you like. They run in
this order:

  Freeze > Repeat > Reverse > Scatter > Dive > Crush > Stutter > Dry/Wet
  > Tape Stop / Tape Start > Output

  STUTTER
      A 16-step chopper locked to your song's tempo. LIT STEPS CUT THE
      VOLUME. Click steps to light them. The current step glows yellow.
      SPEED    Step size: 1/2, 1/4, 1/4T, 1/8, 1/8T, 1/16, 1/16T, 1/32,
               1/32T, 1/64 (T = triplet).
      STEPS    Pattern length, 1-16 (steps past it are dimmed).
      DEPTH    How far the volume drops on a lit step.
      SMOOTH   Edge softness: low = hard chop, high = pulsing.

  REPEAT
      Grabs one slice the moment the glitch fires (you hear it go by once)
      and then loops it: "s-s-s-stutter".
      LENGTH   Slice size, 1/2 to 1/64.
      DROP     Pitch change per repeat. Negative = sinking and dying,
               positive = rising panic.

  REVERSE
      Plays each slice backwards right after it goes by.
      LENGTH   Slice size, 1/2 to 1/64.

  SCATTER
      Every slice, maybe jumps back to a random earlier slice, sometimes
      backwards, sometimes an octave down or up. Broken-CD territory.
      SLICE    Slice size, 1/2 to 1/64.
      CHAOS    How often it jumps (0 = never, 100% = every slice) and how
               often a jump gets reversed or pitched.
      RANGE    How far back it can jump, 1 to 8 beats.

  CRUSH
      BITS     Bit depth, 16 (clean) down to 2 (destroyed).
      RATE     Sample rate, 24 kHz down to 400 Hz (aliasing, robot grit).

  FREEZE
      Holds the moment as a drifting, slightly detuned drone under the
      sound.
      SIZE     Grain length: short = buzzy, long = smeared.
      MIX      How loud the frozen sound is.

  DIVE
      The pitch slides down (or up) while the glitch is held, then slides
      back home after you let go.
      AMOUNT   -24 to +24 semitones.
      TIME     How long the slide takes.

  TAPE STOP
      Everything slows down and drops in pitch until it grinds to silence.
      TIME     How long it takes to stop.
      CURVE    Low = drops fast then drags out, high = holds speed then
               crashes.
      Let go and it snaps straight back (unless TAPE START is armed).

  TAPE START
      Spins up from a dead stop to full speed.
      TIME / CURVE   Same idea as Tape Stop, in reverse.

      TAPE STOP + TAPE START both armed: the tape stops when you press and
      spins back up when you let go. That's the classic DJ "power down,
      power up".

  IN / OUT
      DRY/WET  Blend of your untouched track and the glitched one (Tape
               Stop/Start always act on the whole sound).
      OUTPUT   Final level. Meters show input and output.


===============================================================================
CAPTURE TRAY  (bottom)
===============================================================================
Every time THE BUTTON fires, Glitch Witch records what comes out of it as a
24-bit WAV file and drops it in the tray. The newest clip is on the left.
A red CAPTURING tile shows while a clip is being recorded.

  CAPTURE    On/off switch for recording clips.
  PRE        Clean audio kept BEFORE the glitch: Off, 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2,
             1 Bar, 2 Bars.
  POST       Audio kept AFTER the glitch and its tails (tape spin-up,
             dive sliding home) finish. Same choices.
             The extra audio gives you room to crossfade the clip with the
             original track.
  FOLDER     Opens the folder the WAV files are saved in:
               macOS:   ~/Music/Glitch Witch Captures/<date>/
               Windows: Music\Glitch Witch Captures\<date>\
             Files are named after the track, e.g.
               "Drums - Glitch 007 - Bar 33.1.wav"
             (the track name shows up in DAWs that share it with plugins).
  CLEAR      Empties the tray. You choose whether to keep or delete the files.
  < >        Page through older clips (the tray remembers the last 48).

  Each clip shows its number, its length and where it STARTS in your song:
  "BAR 33 . 1" means bar 33, beat 1. Clips captured with the song stopped
  say TRANSPORT STOPPED.

  Drag a clip out of the tray and drop it on a track in your DAW.
  Right-click a clip to show it in Finder/Explorer, remove it from the tray
  (the file stays on disk) or delete the file.

  If dragging out of the plugin window doesn't work in your DAW, click
  FOLDER and drag the WAV from Finder/Explorer instead. That always works.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BOUNCING GLITCHES TO A NEW TRACK  (Fender Studio Pro)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Put Glitch Witch on the track. Arm your effects, set SNAP (1/4 is a
     good start) and set PRE and POST (1/4 each is a good start).
  2. Play the song and hit THE BUTTON where you want glitches. Or record
     the button as automation first and let playback do the pressing.
  3. Each glitch lands in the tray, labelled with the bar and beat it
     starts on.
  4. Make a new audio track. Turn Snap ON in Studio Pro.
  5. Drag the clip from the tray onto the new track, at the bar and beat
     on its tile. With SNAP and PRE both on in Glitch Witch, the clip starts
     exactly on that grid line, so it snaps into place.
  6. On the original track, turn the glitch off (bypass Glitch Witch, or
     delete the button automation) and cut or fade out the original audio
     under the glitch. Use the PRE/POST overlap for crossfades.

  Any other DAW works the same way: drag the clip to the bar on its tile.

  Extra for DAWs that read Broadcast WAV timestamps:
     Every clip also stores where it came from in the song (a Broadcast
     WAV "time reference"). In REAPER, drop the clip anywhere and use
     Item > "Move to source preferred position" to put it back exactly
     where it was captured. Other DAWs call this "spot to original time"
     or "move to origin".

  Note: if snap is off, clips start wherever you pressed, so the tile
  shows an offset ("BAR 33 . 1 +2/16"). Line those up by ear, or use the
  timestamp trick above.
